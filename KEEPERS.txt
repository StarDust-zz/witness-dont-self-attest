Object (7/7): A store cannot vouch for itself across its own revert. On recheck-after-forget's 88-case matrix, the wm+home gate's 4 residual wrong sends stay at 4 when the head attestation comes from a snapshot-restored counter or a lagging second home, and drop to 0 with an attestation that does not go back with the store (monotone counter, independent replica, append-only witness). Those refuse exactly the 4 residual cases: correct sends stay 14, correct holds stay 2.

Keepers in plain words:
1. Self-attestation follows the snapshot. The same reverted bytes (seq 3) are attested as newest by a counter restored with the data and as stale by a counter kept outside it (head 7). Whether the store can vouch depends on whether its counter shares the data's failure, not on the store.
2. A second home that fails the same way just agrees. The lagging cartridge returns the same seq 3 as the reverted retention home: zero gain, never certifies. Cross-reading helps only when the second home is independent and at least as fresh.
3. Attest at send, not only at certify. Checked only at certify, every independent source still sends 2 wrong drafts (a write landing between certify and send). A frozen witness head that moved (32 cases, exactly the between-writes) is a failed certificate: re-certify, never a silent pass.
4. Refuse is a separate verdict from hold. Hold means a fresh read disagrees with the draft (redraft). Refuse means the home is older than the attested head, so no read from it can certify anything.
5. Revert vs lag, for hold vs escalate: a lag never moves the store's own counter backwards; a revert does once you have seen the newer head. Escalate at once on a backward step, hold and retry on a lag. With no backward step seen, fall back to a retry budget. Hold-forever never ends on a persistent revert.

Non-keepers / partly falsified:
- "A clean matrix certifies the witness" is false. The gain is 1/22 over 88 cases, all in the revert cell. RouteGuard's Bernstein bracket (delta 0.05) refuses at 88 (m* = 274) and in the 22-case revert cell (m* = 64). In the toy the gain is deterministic; the bracket is about what a deployer could claim from this many cases.
- Excess over independence (refuse-to-certify's redundancy signal) is negative for both second homes (-123/1936 lagging, -41/1936 independent). It does not separate redundant from useful here; G and Phi do. Transferring E as the refusal rule is falsified in this setting.
- self/monotone, cross/replica and witness give identical numbers here: they read the same true head. They differ only in which failures they share with the store, which this pack does not model.
- The witness freeze changes no verdict beyond the per-path check at send (both fire on the same cases). Its value here is the label: a moved head is reported as a failed certificate.
- The hold-vs-escalate result (scenario 6) is a stylized trace (catch-up 2, budget 3, horizon 8); the tick numbers are by construction, the ordering is the keeper.

Still a hunch: where an independent monotone witness comes from in practice (an append-only log the store cannot restore, a second replica in a different failure domain, signed write receipts); its read cost on every send; whether a witness that can itself lag (not revert) only delays sends or also causes wrong refusals; and how many real revert-prone sends a deployer needs before the refusal policy itself certifies.
